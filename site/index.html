ENDEFRIT

Easy-language drafts of official texts

A language model writes, code checks, you decide.

Leicht·Facile drafts easy-language versions of official texts from Switzerland, Germany and Austria, and proves with code that no deadline, amount or phone number got lost. It is a drafting assistant for the people who write official texts, not a replacement for their review or for the test by readers from the target group.

What the law asks of authorities

Easy language is not a courtesy. All three countries have ratified the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, which counts plain language as a form of communication (art. 2) and asks states to make public information accessible (art. 9 and 21).

  • Switzerland

    Bound by the Convention since 20142. The Disability Equality Act asks authorities to take the needs of people with disabilities into account when they communicate with the public (art. 14 BehiG3).

  • Germany

    Federal authorities should explain their decisions, forms and contracts in simple language on request, and in Leichte Sprache when that is not enough (§ 11 BGG4). Every federal website explains itself in Leichte Sprache on its home page (§ 4 BITV 2.05).

  • Austria

    Bound by the Convention since 20086. The Federal Disability Equality Act counts information systems among what must be accessible to people with disabilities without special difficulty (§ 6 BGStG7). No statute names Leichte Sprache itself.

How it works

The language model does everything that needs language understanding. Plain code does everything that can be counted.

  1. Understand model

    The document becomes a fact sheet: the reader's core questions, every fact with its exact value, hard words. Code adds every date, amount and contact the model missed.

  2. Write model

    A draft in easy language, every sentence linked to the source sentences it comes from, every omission explained.

  3. Check code

    Deterministic rules from the published guidelines of Switzerland, Germany and Austria, 45 for German, 41 for French and 41 for Italian, and an exact check of every fact value in both directions: no value lost, no value invented.

  4. Judge model

    Meaning only: distorted facts, invented statements, metaphors, unclear references.

  5. Repair model

    Only the flagged sentences, until no blocking finding is left; then one sentence for each must-keep value still missing.

  6. Review person

    Source and draft side by side, provenance, fact checklist; approve, edit or reject each block, export with an audit trail.

Why not one chatbot prompt?

Ask a chatbot to "rewrite this in Leichte Sprache" and you get a text, nothing more.

  • No fact contract

    Nothing says which deadlines, amounts and contacts must survive.

  • No provenance

    No sentence points back to the source sentence it comes from.

  • No omission log

    What was left out, and why, is not recorded.

  • No independent check

    Nothing but the model itself looks at the result.

The chatbots people reach for also run on foreign clouds. Leicht·Facile adds those four things, a review workflow and an audit trail, and runs on infrastructure the organisation controls. The next section measures the difference, and what it costs.

Your model, your infrastructure

Leicht·Facile works with any OpenAI-compatible endpoint: a provider's API, a gateway or a model server of your own. Documents and review records stay in a database in a folder the organisation controls. At runtime the app contacts only the model endpoint, and a web page's server when a user enters a URL: no telemetry, no CDN, no login provider.

Why Apertus for confidential texts

A letter that names a person is under official secrecy, a patient letter under medical secrecy, a tax letter under tax secrecy. Such texts need a model that runs where the organisation controls it, with open weights and open training data. Apertus10, by EPFL, ETH Zurich and CSCS, meets all of that, and Leicht·Facile was built and evaluated with it (why Apertus).

Get started

With uv installed, one command downloads the app from PyPI, starts it and opens the browser:

uvx leichtfacile serve

The app's model page asks for the endpoint and its key: Apertus on the CSCS inference API, the Public AI gateway, a provider such as Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or OpenRouter, or a server of your own (vLLM, llama.cpp, Ollama).

Would your administration like help setting it up? See Support for public administrations.

Roadmap

What has shipped since work began in October 2026, and what comes next. Ideas and requests are welcome as GitHub issues. If your administration needs something from the list sooner, or something that is not on it, tell us.

  1. 0.5 to 0.9 October 2026

    Shipped

    • 0.5 and 0.6 German and French: Leichte Sprache, Einfache Sprache and FALC, the fact sheet, the rule checks, the judge, the repair loop and the review page; exports as Markdown, HTML and Word with an audit trail; the decision mode for KESB and court decisions; the evaluation on 20 held-out pairs (results above).
    • 0.7 Any model, one command: uvx leichtfacile serve from PyPI, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, presets for Apertus and the large providers.
    • 0.8 Italian: lingua facile with 28 rules of its own, a tagger built from the Morph-it! lexicon, an evaluation on Public AI; the app's model page.
    • 0.9 German for Germany and Austria: the rules of the Netzwerk Leichte Sprache and DIN SPEC 33429, each country's spelling, numbers, dates, phone numbers and addresses, the Bescheid in the decision mode; evaluated on 8 official pages a country.

  2. Planned

    French for France and Italian for Italy

    FALC and lingua facile as the two countries write them, built like the German varieties: each country's rule sources, its numbers, euro amounts, dates, phone numbers, addresses and identifiers, the words of its authorities, prompts, sample letters and an evaluation on official pages. The Swiss French and Swiss Italian stay unchanged.

  3. Planned

    Letters someone else wrote

    The person who received a letter, or someone who helps them, uploads it. Code lists every deadline, amount and contact in the letter's own wording, a fixed notice says that only the letter counts, and the explanation gives no advice.

  4. Planned

    Decisions: detection and long reasoning

    The app recognises the type of document instead of asking for the mode, and a further mode condenses long legal reasoning and lists every omission.

  5. Planned

    More kinds of documents

    Discharge letters, lease terminations, penal orders, payment orders, school decisions and information for study participants, each with the periods and values its readers must not miss (use cases).

  6. Research

    Scanned letters and photos

    Apertus 1.5 reads images, but on whole pages it turned IBANs and periods into other plausible numbers. Sent in strips at the scan's resolution, it kept every digit of two test letters. For now, a PDF without a text layer gets a clear error message.

Support for public administrations

Leicht·Facile is free and open source, and you can run it on your own. If you work for a public administration and would like help, we, both authors, support you on a voluntary basis and free of charge:

  • Putting it into operation

    Installation on your servers or in your cloud, connection to your model endpoint (Apertus on your own hardware, the CSCS inference API, Public AI or another provider), hardware sizing, and air-gapped installation, where the app, the model and all data run in a network with no connection to the internet.

  • Adjustment to your texts

    Your terms and vocabulary, the facts your letters must never lose, your house rules for easy language, your types of documents.

  • Integration

    Connection to your document management or case management system.

  • Evaluation with your own texts

    We measure the drafts on your letters and their easy-language versions, with the method behind the results above, so you see what the app does before you start.

Tell us briefly who you are, which texts and languages you have in mind and where the app should run, and we will get back to you.
